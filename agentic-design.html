Automation Agents

I design AI agents that work alongside users as they build and run their business on Thryv CRM. Each agent takes on a different job, from naming nodes to configuring complex rules. This case study starts with the Decision Diamond, now live in production.

Feature 1

AI-assisted Decision Diamond

⚠️ The Decision Diamond decides which path a workflow takes, but it only checks contact details. Small businesses run on jobs, estimates and appointments, so owners linked three flows with tags just to route an estimate by value. Discoverability was the other friction. The Decision Diamond only appears when a user connects two downstream nodes to one upstream node, so it stays hidden on the canvas. Fixing both would unleash the builder's capacity and align it with business goals, turning an underused feature into a driver of adoption and revenue.

Solution

The Decision Diamond now reads deals, quotes and appointments, and AI auto-reads upstream nodes to best suggest the rule to divert the qualified cohort to the next path. This is a huge milestone for the team to move forward to the Builder Agent that interprets plain language into workflows.

Achievements

  • Led the redesign from concept to production.
  • Picked up the PM hat to spec priority entities.
  • Tested the redesign with partners and small business owners on a live LLM prototype, not scripted answers.
  • Measured completion rate for discoverability, and completion time and error rate for quality.

AI Design Framework

I ran a workshop with product and engineering to collect their ideas for AI in the builder, and interviewed our pilot group for what they actually wanted. Together that came to 48 requests across five themes, from discovery and guidance to pre-launch checks. Every one sounded reasonable on its own, and they piled up faster than we could scope them.

Alpha testing

In alpha, the Decision Diamond reads the nodes upstream of it to understand what the flow is doing. It uses that context to help users set the rules that divert a designated group to the right downstream nodes.

I built a taxonomy. One axis is the level of autonomy, how much the AI does and how much work is left to the user, from executing to reviewing to approving. The other is reach, from a focused config change to a platform wide agent. Three things decide where a feature lands: how users work today, how mature our model is for that task, and how much reliance the output can reasonably carry. Routing logic in the Decision Diamond changes where work goes, so a person reviews each rule before it takes effect. Auto renaming a sequence is small enough to check at a glance, so the guardrail is lighter. The user still reads and confirms it, and it is fast and easy.
